I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities, advised by Prof. Catherine Qi Zhao in the VIP Lab.

I am currently collaborating with Fasikl Incorporated on AI-powered neural decoding using neural computing interface and with Google DeepMind on federated learning for distributed networks.

Before joining the Ph.D. program, I received an M.S. in Computer Science from Georgia State University (2022), where I was advised by Prof. Donghyun Kim and Prof. Yingshu Li, and a B.E. in Computer Engineering from Chonnam National University, South Korea (2020). From May 2020 to April 2020, I worked in the Korea Electric Power Corporation.

My research focuses on building machine learning systems that generalize across distributed, heterogeneous environments — with applications in biomedical signal processing, neural decoding, human-centered AI, and IoT networks. I design federated learning frameworks and neural network architectures that enable efficient, generalizable, and personalized AI for scalable real-world deployment. At the core of this work is a belief that AI should be deeply aligned with human needs: systems that adapt to individual users, operate reliably in clinical settings, and ultimately improve people's quality of life. This human-centered philosophy is reflected in my publications across leading journal venues, and I see my Ph.D. as one chapter in a longer journey — defined by a commitment to bridging principled machine learning research and meaningful real-world impact.

My Ph.D. program is generously supported by the Doctoral Dissertation Fellowship from the University of Minnesota.
